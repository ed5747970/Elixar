Elixar User Guide

The cure for a cluttered PC · made by Elisar · version 1.2.31

Elixar frees space on drive C: safely. It shows what fills the drive, from Critical to Safe, and removes only what you choose.

At a glance

Start on the Dashboard to see what fills C:, then clean in the Cleaner; the other tabs handle apps, deeper tools, your phone and schedules. Press F1 in Elixar any time to open this guide.

Getting started

Unzip Elixar.zip anywhere (for example your Desktop). Elixar needs only Windows 10 or 11; nothing else to install.

  1. Check the download (optional). Open a Command Prompt in the folder and run certutil -hashfile Elixar.exe SHA256. The result must match SHA256.txt.
  2. Run the safe self-test. Double-click “Run self-test (safe).bat”. It works only in its own Temp folder, measures your real items without deleting anything, opens the window with cleaning switched off, and saves selftest-report.txt. If Windows shows “Windows protected your PC”, click More info › Run anyway.
  3. Open Elixar. Double-click Elixar.exe and click Yes on the Windows prompt (it needs administrator rights to clean Windows folders).
  4. Clean. Start on the Dashboard to see what fills C:, then go to the Cleaner tab, click a row to read what it removes, tick what you want and click “Clean selected”. Nothing is deleted before you confirm, and a restore point is made first.

Dashboard

The Dashboard opens first. It tells you at a glance how your drives are doing and what you can do about it. Looking never deletes anything; every clean asks first.

Choosing a drive

The switch at the top left lists C: Windows, every other drive in the PC (and USB drives), and All drives. Refresh (F5), Explore and Folder map work on the drive you picked.

Under the switch, Elixar shows which physical disk the drive is on and this PC's name, for example Disk 0 of ELISAR-PC. The number is the one Windows' Disk Management shows (it starts at 0); two drives on the same disk show the same number. In All drives, every card has its own line.

Your other drives (D:, E:, USB)

Pick a drive in the switch. Its page shows how full it is, whether it is a data drive (no Windows on it) or has its own Windows installation, its biggest folders and largest files, and three buttons:

  • Smart scan: reads the drive once and finds duplicate files (exactly the same content, compared by fingerprint) and big files not used for 6 months (or a year, or 2 years). For duplicates you choose which copy stays: the oldest, the newest or the one with the shortest path. Nothing is ticked for you; what you pick goes to Undo (Tools › Undo puts it back). Windows, apps, games and AppData are never listed. On C:, Smart scan looks at your own folders. It also lists empty folders (removed only if they are still empty, one at a time, so nothing put there since is lost), can compress files you keep but rarely open (Windows' own NTFS compression: the files stay where they are and open as usual; not offered for videos, photos, music or archives, which don't get smaller), and, if you tick Also look for similar photos, finds photos that look alike but aren't exact copies (resized, edited or burst shots), keeping the biggest of each set.
  • Clean D: opens the Cleaner at that drive's own group: its Recycle Bin, pieces left by a disk check (found.000), and Steam's shader cache and unfinished downloads in game libraries there. Nothing is ticked for you, and the weekly clean never touches other drives.
  • Explore: the folder drill-down for that drive.

A USB stick has no Recycle Bin: files removed from it are deleted for good, and Elixar warns you first. In Tools › Duplicates, Add a whole drive… searches any drive, or all of them at once (it also finds copies spread across drives). The low-space alert now covers C: and every internal drive.

What grew this week, and the report

Once a day Elixar writes down the size of each place on the Dashboard map (sizes only, on this PC). After a week the Dashboard says what grew the most, for example “Downloads folder grew 4.4 GB this week”, and the Space trend window lists it. Export report in the footer saves a one-page summary (drives, what fills C:, what can be cleaned, what grew) as a web page; press Ctrl+P and choose “Microsoft Print to PDF” to keep it as a PDF. It holds sizes and folder names only, never your file names or email address.

PIN or password

Elixar can ask for a PIN (4, 6 or 8 digits) or a password (8 to 64 characters) every time it opens, and when it comes back from the icon next to the clock. It's offered once the first time Elixar opens; turn it on or off with Secure your data with a PIN or password in Settings › Security and protection (unticking asks for the PIN first), and change it there. When you set it, Elixar shows a recovery code once: keep it, it's the only way to remove a forgotten PIN. After 5 wrong tries you wait 30 seconds, then twice as long each time (up to 15 minutes), also after a restart. The PIN is kept only as a slow salted hash in a folder only administrators can open. It stops other people who use your PC from opening Elixar; it doesn't encrypt your files, and the scheduled clean, daily tidy and emails keep running without it.

Folders to watch

Elixar writes down the size of each place on the Dashboard once a day (sizes only, never file names). After two weeks it knows which folders always grow (in at least 3 of every 4 weeks) and which grow fast (2 GB a month or more). The Watch tile on the Dashboard shows the one that most needs a look; click it for the full list: how much each one grows a month, what to do about it, and how often to look (every week from 5 GB a month, every 2 weeks from 1 GB, otherwise every month). After you look, click I checked it: the folder stays quiet until its next look is due. The weekly email and the exported report list them too.

The tabs

The tabs run along the top: Dashboard, Apps, Cleaner, Tools, Phone, Email and Automation, with Settings on the right (the gear). A Linux tab is shown greyed out: it only works when Elixar runs on Linux.

C: Overview

  • The ring: how full C: is, the free space in the middle, and one plain sentence: in good shape, getting full (under 20% or 25 GB free) or almost full (under 10% or 8 GB). It also warns when the space trend says C: fills up within a year.
  • Free … safely: cleans only the Safe items that are ticked by default (the same set the weekly clean uses): caches and temp files that Windows and your apps rebuild by themselves. It lists them and asks before deleting. They are deleted, not kept in Undo.
  • What fills C:: safe to clean, your files, leftovers and system stores, Windows and apps, and free space.
  • Six tiles (click one to open it): Disk health, Space trend (with a small chart), Last clean, Automation, Email reports and Undo.
  • Quick wins: the five biggest safe items, each with its own Clean button (it asks first).
  • Other drives and Recent activity (cleans, weekly cleans, emails sent and moves to Undo).

  • Find largest files: scans the whole drive (2 to 5 minutes, read-only) and lists files over 50 MB from Critical to Safe. Double-click a file to show it in its folder; right-click › Uninstall when it belongs to an app.
  • Folder drill-down: double-click a folder row or click Explore to see what is inside, biggest first. Backspace goes up.

Another drive

Pick D:, E: and so on to see how full it is, what fills it, its biggest folders (Open shows what is inside) and, when you ask, its largest files (this reads the whole drive, so it only runs when you click it; Show opens the file's folder). If Disk health says a drive may fail, Elixar doesn't read through it on its own: back up first.

All drives

Every drive side by side, with its free space, kind and health, and which one has the most room. When C: is getting full and another drive has room, it offers Move files off C:. Click a drive's card to open it.

  • Space trend: free space over time and a forecast like “C: loses 5 GB a month, full around October 2027”. Elixar records one reading a day, so the chart fills in over a few days.
  • Folder map: boxes sized by the space they use and coloured by level. Click a folder to go inside; Backspace goes back.

Cleaner

The Cleaner lists 43 items in four groups; tick what you want and click “Clean selected”. Click any row to read exactly what it removes. Free space for me... asks how many GB you want back and ticks the safest items that reach it (Caution items and the Recycle Bin only if you allow them; privacy items never).

  • Rescan (F5): measures again. The Change column shows how much each item grew (orange) or shrank (green) since the last scan.

Apps

Uninstalling apps you don't use is the safe way to shrink the Critical part of C:.

Programs: every installed program with its size on disk and when it was last used (from Windows' own records). Orange means no use found in 90 days. Click a column to sort. “Uninstall...” runs the app's own uninstaller, the same one Windows uses; apps installed for your account only are uninstalled with your normal rights, not as administrator.

Tools

The Tools tab has seven inner tabs for deeper jobs.

Disk health

Reads the health, wear and error counters your drives keep about themselves (the same data Windows uses) and tells you, in plain words, before a drive fails. It only reads: nothing is written to any drive, nothing is downloaded, nothing is sent anywhere, and serial numbers are not read.

ResultWhenWhat to do
HealthyWindows says the drive is fine and nothing below appliesNothing
Needs attentionWindows reports a warning; under 20% of an SSD's rated write life left; errors the drive couldn't fix; running hot (55 °C for a hard disk, 70 °C for an SSD)Keep a recent backup and check again in a few days (or let a hot PC cool down)
May fail soonWindows says unhealthy; the drive's own S.M.A.R.T. check predicts failure; its rated write life is used upBack up your files today, then replace the drive
UnknownThe drive shares nothing (common for USB drives)Never shown as healthy when Elixar can't tell

Wear, temperature and error counts need administrator rights, which Elixar normally has. The daily low-space check also checks the drives and shows a notification: every day while a drive may fail soon, once a week while it needs attention.

Drivers

Shows this PC's hardware (CPU, graphics, motherboard, disks, network) and finds drivers it doesn't need.

Drivers a device uses, drivers Windows needs to start, and printer and extension drivers are never offered. Each removed driver is backed up to Undo first.

Startup

Everything that starts with Windows, checked for who signed it and where it runs from. “Needs a look” flags hidden scripts, programs in Temp or Downloads, and broken or missing signatures. Turn entries off or on (the same switch as Task Manager), remove leftovers whose program is gone (kept in Undo), or scan any of them with Microsoft Defender. Not signed doesn't mean harmful; many small tools aren't signed.

System space

  • Hibernation file: hiberfil.sys is often 6-16 GB. “Make smaller” keeps Fast Startup but drops Hibernate; “Turn off” frees all of it; “Turn back on” restores it.
  • WinSxS: see what DISM can really clean, then clean it.
  • Reserved storage: turn off to free about 7 GB (turn it on again before big updates).
  • Restore points: see how much space they use and set a limit.
  • Compact OS: Windows compresses its own files (about 2 GB), reversible.
  • WSL and Docker disks: shrink virtual disks that only ever grow.

Move off C:

Moves Downloads, Documents, Pictures, Videos, Music and Desktop to your second drive the way Windows does it (Properties › Location): every file is copied and checked, then Windows is pointed at the new place, then the old copies are deleted. “Move back to C:” undoes it. Folders kept in sync by OneDrive are left to OneDrive. Steam games on C: are listed by size; Steam moves them itself (Settings › Storage › Move). Buttons open Windows’ settings for moving Store apps and for saving new files on another drive.

Duplicates

Finds files with exactly the same content in Documents, Pictures, Videos, Music, Desktop, Downloads or folders you add. Windows, apps and AppData are never searched. Extra copies go to Undo (kept for the days you chose in Automation) and at least one copy of each file is always kept.

Leftovers

Folders in AppData and ProgramData left behind by apps you removed. A folder is listed only when no installed app, publisher, service, program that starts with Windows or running program matches its name, nothing in it changed for 3 months, and it holds at least 1 MB. Folders of Windows, drivers, browsers, games platforms and security programs are never listed, and neither are folders named with a code instead of a name. These are guesses, so nothing is ticked: look first (double-click opens a folder). What you pick goes to Undo for 7 days, so you can put it back.

Sysprep

Gets this PC ready for Sysprep before you capture an image (Sysprep with Generalize). Check this PC lists what would stop Sysprep and offers Microsoft's fix for each; nothing changes without asking, and a restore point is made before a fix:

  • Stops Sysprep: BitLocker on the Windows drive (Turn BitLocker off: it decrypts in the background); Store apps installed for one account only (Remove them for all accounts); Windows upgraded in place (Microsoft's workaround, 3 registry values; a clean install is the supported way); Sysprep's state left from an earlier run or too many runs (Reset Sysprep's state).
  • Fix first: a restart Windows is waiting for; little free space (clean first).
  • Good to know: Store app updates (pause them while you prepare, turn them back on afterwards), other accounts on the PC, domain membership, errors from the last Sysprep run (Sysprep's log is read and explained).

Open Sysprep… only opens Windows' Sysprep: choose Enter System Out-of-Box Experience (OOBE), tick Generalize, and Shutdown there.

Golden image

Besides what stops Sysprep, Check this PC looks for what would be copied to every PC made from the image. Elixar only checks whether a password is there; it never reads it.

  • Saved passwords and keys: a password saved for automatic sign-in (Winlogon or Sysinternals Autologon), Wi-Fi passwords, saved sign-ins in Credential Manager, old answer files that Windows Setup would read before yours (in Windows\Panther or on a USB drive), saved browser passwords (Chrome, Edge, Brave, Vivaldi, Opera, Firefox, counted per account), SSH keys and command-line cloud sign-ins (AWS, Azure, Google Cloud, Kubernetes, Docker, Git, npm), and Elixar's own email sign-in. Elixar removes the first four and its email sign-in after asking; the others are listed for you to move or delete.
  • Programs with their own ID: management, security and remote-support agents (for example Configuration Manager, Intune, CrowdStrike, SentinelOne, Defender for Endpoint, Symantec, Kaspersky, TeamViewer, AnyDesk) make an ID when installed. Each one is listed with the vendor's golden-image step. A PC joined to Microsoft Entra ID or enrolled in Intune is flagged too.
  • How to build: a virtual machine is reminded to take a checkpoint before Sysprep; Restart into Audit mode runs sysprep /audit /reboot; Copy mine to new accounts copies your Start layout and default apps to the default account with Windows' own tools (Windows' original files are kept as .before-elixar copies). With CopyProfile in the answer file, extra accounts are flagged, so the right one is copied.

The Golden image buttons:

  • Image cleanup… removes what an image doesn't need: Windows Update downloads, caches, temp files, error reports, crash dumps, servicing logs, the Recycle Bin, old Windows and upgrade leftovers, a final component cleanup (DISM /ResetBase: installed updates can no longer be removed), event logs, the hibernation file (off by default) and restore points (always last). Each line shows its size and what it does; nothing runs without your confirmation, and nothing in it can be undone.
  • Image passport… saves an HTML report of the image: Windows version and build, the checklist, the answer file (without passwords or the product key), programs, Store apps for new accounts, updates and added drivers. Keep it next to the image to know what is in each version.
  • Capture kit… writes Capture-Image.cmd, Split-Image.cmd and a README to a USB drive. After Sysprep shuts the PC down, start it from Windows PE or a Windows installation USB (Shift+F10) and run the kit: it finds the Windows partition, asks for a name (letters, digits and _ only) and captures it with DISM, compressed and verified. Split-Image makes parts under 4 GB for FAT32 media.

Answer file (unattend.xml)

Answer file… makes, loads and checks the answer file that sets up each PC made from your image by itself, without anyone clicking through Windows' setup screens:

  • This PC: computer name (* = a random name for each PC), owner, organization, product key (optional), time zone.
  • Language: Windows language, system locale, formats, keyboards (names like en-US or he-IL).
  • Setup screens to skip: license, manufacturer registration, Microsoft account screens (use a local account), Wi-Fi, privacy questions.
  • Account: a local administrator account with a password, and signing in to it automatically once.
  • Built-in Administrator: Sysprep turns it off. Tick Turn on the built-in Administrator after setup and give it a password: the password is set by Windows Setup, and the account is turned on at the first sign-in. It is found by its ID, so this works in every Windows language. A password alone (without the tick) leaves the account off.
  • Image: CopyProfile (copy this account's settings to every new account), keep installed drivers, SkipRearm.
  • First sign-in: commands that run once (for example installing an app), and Delete the answer file after setup (on by default).

Check reads the file the way Windows Setup does: wrong names, product keys, languages, time zones, setup passes, duplicate parts, parts made for another processor type, and settings in the wrong pass. Problems that would stop Setup are red and block saving. The built-in Administrator can't be turned on without a password, or with Delete the answer file after setup off. Save to Sysprep's folder writes C:\Windows\System32\Sysprep\unattend.xml, which Sysprep uses by itself; Save as… writes anywhere. Load… opens an existing file (settings this editor doesn't show are kept out and you are told which; save under a new name to keep the original). Run Sysprep with it… saves, shows the exact command (Generalize, OOBE, Shutdown, /mode:vm for a virtual machine) and runs it only after you confirm.

Safety: a password in an answer file is only encoded (Base64), never encrypted, so anyone who can read the file can read it. Elixar doesn't keep it anywhere else, and Delete the answer file after setup removes the file from the new PC at its first sign-in. Outside files are read with web links and DTDs turned off, and files over 4 MB are refused.

Undo

Files you remove yourself (Smart scan, Duplicates) are kept here for 7, 30 or 90 days, your choice in Automation (30 by default). Caution items, removed drivers and removed startup entries are kept for 7 days. Undo uses your second drive when there is one, so C: space is freed right away. Restore puts everything back exactly where it was, also files from a USB drive (which has no Recycle Bin); when its time runs out, an item is deleted for good.

Automation

These schedules run through Windows Task Scheduler even when Elixar is closed; click Save to turn them on or off.

  • Low-space alert: a Windows notification when C: drops under the limit you choose (default 15%), checked daily and a few minutes after you sign in. The same check warns you when a drive's health gets worse.
  • Weekly auto-clean: cleans only the 11 default Safe items on the day and time you choose. Never Caution items, the Recycle Bin, browser caches, privacy items or DISM. Open apps are skipped, never closed, and a notification shows what was freed.
  • Auto-delete switch on the Dashboard: the Auto-delete tile has an on/off switch. On: the daily tidy below runs (old Recycle Bin items, Undo, a clean when C: is low); Elixar asks once before turning it on. Off: nothing is deleted by itself. Click the rest of the tile for the details here.
  • Recycle Bin and Undo (the “daily tidy”): every day, Elixar empties only what has been in your Recycle Bin for more than 14, 30, 60 or 90 days, on every internal drive, and only your own Recycle Bin. What you deleted recently stays. It also deletes Undo items whose time is up, and, if you tick it, runs the Safe clean right away when C: drops under your alert limit (at most once a day). Empty old Recycle Bin items now… does the same at once. The section shows how much of your Recycle Bin is old.
  • Keep files you remove in Undo for: 7, 30 or 90 days (see Undo, under Tools).
Schedules start the protected copy in C:\Program Files\Elixar. After updating Elixar, open Automation and click Save once so they use the new version.

Settings and updates

Open the Settings tab (or click Settings in the footer). Pick a language and a theme, then click Apply and restart Elixar: Elixar restarts for a moment in the new look. The small picture next to the theme choices shows what it will look like.

  • Language: English or Hebrew (the window is laid out right to left). Some live status lines stay in English.
  • Theme: follow Windows, light or dark.
  • Install Elixar on this PC: adds it to the Start menu and to Windows’ installed apps list (with Uninstall), in C:\Program Files\Elixar.
  • Check for updates: once Elixar is published on GitHub, it checks for a new version (also quietly once a week). An update is installed only if it is signed with Elisar’s key and every file matches its fingerprint.

OneDrive

The OneDrive tab appears only when OneDrive is signed in on this PC with your account email. Elixar doesn't sign in or connect to anything: it reads OneDrive's own settings on this PC. The Dashboard also suggests it when files you haven't used for 3 months add up to 1 GB or more.

It lists files of 1 MB and up that are on this PC and that you haven't opened for 1, 3, 6 or 12 months. Make selected online-only does the same as OneDrive's own “Free up space”: the files stay in OneDrive and in File Explorer, but take no space here until you open one (OneDrive then downloads it, which needs internet). Nothing is deleted. Files marked “Always keep on this device” are left alone, nothing is ticked for you, and reading the folder never downloads anything. Elixar changes only real OneDrive files inside your OneDrive folder (each must carry OneDrive's own cloud mark), so even a changed OneDrive setting can't point it at other files.

Security

Elixar runs as administrator, so since 1.7 it guards against a virus with normal rights tricking it into doing admin work.

To check your copy, click “Verify this copy” in the footer: it shows Elixar's SHA-256 fingerprint to compare with SHA256.txt, and the state of the protected copy and the Undo folder. Elixar is not an antivirus.
